The criterion comparison also shows where the updated Sentra benchmark is already strong: its clarity and technical-depth scores sit at 9/10, while feasibility is 8/10. Any claim that another project “beats Sentra” therefore needs to specify the criterion or product evidence; the overall workbook does not support a blanket claim.

## Interpretation limits

The top-75 table is a workbook comparison, not a fresh independent ranking. The workbook itself notes that Sentra was updated after a follow-up repositioning strategy, while the associated source abstract is older. No credit has been added for the current website or MVP in the calculated deltas. In Round 2, a working demo can change judges’ evaluation, but that would be a new live-judging assessment rather than a revision of these workbook numbers.

## Non-biased interpretation

The workbook supports **three narrow conclusions**. First, Sanchari is the only in-scope submission with a higher recorded total than the updated Sentra entry, and it leads by three points through higher innovation and impact rather than feasibility, clarity, or technical depth. Second, RetainIQ is tied on total and has a one-point impact advantage, while Sentra has a one-point feasibility advantage. Third, the table does not establish that Sentra is automatically better than the remaining 72 entries, because a different criterion, user value, or later live demonstration could matter to judges.

The source texts also impose a limit on any Sentra claim. The original Sentra abstract promises budget, intent, and risk checks with ALLOW, ESCALATE, and DENY outcomes. It does not itself prove the later v2 repositioning or the current working MVP. Therefore, this report does not silently convert implementation claims into workbook points. Any Round 2 advantage must be earned through a live, repeatable demonstration in front of judges.
